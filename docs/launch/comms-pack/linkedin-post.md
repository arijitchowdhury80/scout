## Launch Post (Day 0)

Today I'm putting Scout in front of everyone.

Here's the problem it solves. Most web tools hand you a wall of text. You still have to read it, clean it, and remember which page each fact came from. Nine times out of ten, that last part gets lost. Then you write a report built on facts you can't actually point to.

Scout gives you records instead of blobs.

Point it at a website and you get the company details, the people, the open roles, the funding history, and the recent news, each one sitting in its own field. For product teams, it turns an online store into clean product records that load straight into search. And here's the part I care about most: every fact keeps a link back to the exact page it came from. You can always show your work.

You reach it two ways. A simple web API you can call from anything. And a skill that plugs into Claude and Codex, so your AI assistant can go read the web and bring back real records instead of guesses.

It's live on Product Hunt today, and it's a beta. I'm opening 50 spots. I want 50 people who work with web data to use it hard and tell me where it breaks. I would genuinely rather hear the ugly feedback now.

If that's you, the link is in the comments. And if you know someone who lives in scraping and research, tag them.

## Follow-Up Post (Day 3 or 4)

Quick update on the Scout launch, and one thing I got wrong.

The response has been generous. But the most useful thing wasn't the sign-ups. It was the people who told me exactly where the tool stumbled on their own websites. A few sites broke it in ways I hadn't seen. That's the whole reason I ran a beta instead of a big reveal.

One pattern keeps coming up in the feedback. People don't want another tool that dumps text. They want the source attached so they can trust what they're handing to a client or a model. That was the bet behind Scout, and it's holding.

Still looking for a handful more beta testers who work with web data day to day. If you crawl sites, build research, or prep product catalogs, I'd like your eyes on it. Link in the comments.

Thank you to everyone who has already broken something and told me about it. That's the gift.
